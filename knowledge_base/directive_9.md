# Directive 9

Directive 9, also known as Vat-born Protocol 66, Protocol 66, or simply " the Order," was one of the top-secret contingency orders that identified all Vethari as traitors to the Stellar Commonwealth and, therefore, subject to summary execution by the Grand Army of the Commonwealth. The order was programmed into the Grand Army vat soldiers through behavioral modification biochips implanted in their brains from creation, making it almost impossible for the vat-born to disobey the command to turn against their Vethari Generals. The Rainspirian scientists who designed the vat soldiers believed it was only to be used as a contingency protocol against renegade Vethari. In secret, Directive 9 was the means by which the Korrun intended to bring about the long-awaited fall of the Vethari Circle.
During the Vat Wars, the Korrun agenda was progressing relatively unnoticed until a premature activation of Directive 9 resulted in the death of General Tiplar at the hands of Tup, a defective vat-born whose malfunctioning chip caused him to betray his Vethari officer. The incident led ARC trooper Fives to conduct his own investigation into the matter, believing the incident to be a Secessionist plot against the Commonwealth. On the stellar capital Halcyon Prime, however, he learned that the chips conditioned the vat-born to become hostile against the Vethari. Furthermore, he discovered to an extent Supreme Praetor Orlan Dremmel's involvement in the conspiracy and attempted to assassinate him. Fives told what he knew to his Vethari superior Kaelen Vossmark, but before the vat-born could prove it, he was killed by vat-born shock troopers of the Halcyon Prime Guard.
The plan continued apace for the remainder of the war, although the Vethari High Council had grown increasingly wary of the Praetor's motives. In 19 BF, a group of Vethari Masters sought to arrest Dremmel upon learning his true identity as the Korrun Overlord Vorth Oskaryn. When their efforts failed, Dremmel used the incident to frame the Vethari Circle for treason and, as such, forcibly compelled the vat army to execute their Vethari commanding officers in accordance with Directive 9.
Across the galaxy, thousands of Vethari were betrayed and killed by their own troops while the Praetor's new apprentice, the Korrun Overlord Vorth Velgor, formerly Vethari Warden Kaelen Vossmark, led the 501st Legion in a direct assault on the Vethari Sanctum of Halcyon Prime. With the exception of a few surviving Vethari, such as Grand Master Qelmar, Vethari Elder Orrin Valdane, former Acolyte Ilyra Sendt, Padawans Kanan Jarrus and Cal Kestis, and Vethari Warden Cere Junda, as well as Kelleran Beq and youngling Pim, the Great Vethari Purge was largely accomplished through the initial activation of Directive 9. Even after the initial onslaught, Directive 9 still stood, and all Vethari, even people who were merely suspected of being Vethari, were to be summarily killed on sight, without requiring evidence or explanation as to why the kill was carried out. Oskaryn would go on to consolidate his reign as Sovereign of the Obsidian Dominion, ruling for decades until the prophecy of the Foretold One came to pass, marking the end of the Korrun and the restoration of balance in the Synth Flux by 4 AF.

## Description

The vat soldiers of the Grand Army of the Commonwealth were programmed to comply with Vat-born Protocol 66 during the cloning process. All vat-born were given a behavioral modification biochip that could be activated by remote order via voice command; Supreme Praetor Orlan Dremmel, for example, only needed to tell vat-born to execute Directive 9, and the order was carried out. The biochip, when activated, would ensure total obedience in a vat-born, erasing existing biases and beliefs and instilling absolute loyalty to Dremmel. The order also forced the vat-born to view the Vethari as dangerous traitors who needed to be eliminated at all costs, even their own lives. Officially, the order was part of the one hundred and fifty Executive Order Codes for the Grand Army of the Commonwealth.
In some instances, the vat-born could resist the will of the chip and hesitate from executing his orders. This act took great effort, and resistance could not be maintained unless the organic chip was removed, which required external assistance in the case of Vat-born Commander Rex. A few vat-born were immune to the order, but they were quite rare. Genetically defective vat-born had a high probability of immunity to the command, due to their mutations interfering with the performance of the inhibitor chip. However, the chip remained a danger as long as it was still inside a vat-born's head whether he was immune to the order or not, as outside factors such as head injuries could set it off. Failure to comply with Directive 9 was deemed an act of treason; as such, any vat soldier who defied the order was also subject to execution.

## History


## Prelude

The Korrun, an order of users of the shadow current of the Synth Flux, was formed by fallen members of the Vethari Circle after a war called the Hundred-Year Darkness. Eventually, after many wars, the Vethari defeated and seemingly destroyed the Korrun in the final, decisive conflict. However, one Korrun Overlord survived: Vorth Bane. Believing that the Korrun's infighting had caused their downfall, Bane created a tradition called the Rule of Two where he chose to pass his knowledge on to only one Korrun apprentice, who was required to kill their Korrun Master in order to succeed them.
